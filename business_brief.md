# Business Brief: Sales Trends and Customer Value

**Prepared for:** Head of E-commerce | **Period:** Jan-Dec 2025 | **Data:** simulated (5,000 orders, 400 customers, INR). No real customer data used.

## Context
We wanted to know which months and categories earn the most, who our best customers are, and which customers are going quiet. Total revenue in the period was **INR 2.98 million**, with an average order value of **INR 595**.

## Key findings
- **Revenue climbs through the year and peaks in the festive quarter.** November is the best month (INR 323k), about **1.7x** the weakest month, February (INR 187k). Q4 brings **30.9%** of annual revenue versus 20.6% in Q1. December falls 10.1% from November.
- **Electronics earns almost half the revenue.** Electronics is **45.7%** of revenue from 30% of orders, because each order is worth about INR 912. Fashion has the most orders (1,751) but only 26.6% of revenue. Books is the smallest at 6.0%.
- **Revenue is concentrated, but not exactly 80/20.** The top 10% of customers (40 people) bring **36.2%** of revenue and place about 45 orders each. The top 20% bring 51.5%. Reaching 80% of revenue takes half the customer base (202 customers).
- **Few customers have gone quiet.** 35 customers (**8.8%**) have not ordered in over 90 days. 33 of them are in the bottom half by spend, and none are in the top 10%. Together they account for about INR 96k of past spend (3.2% of revenue).

## Recommendations
1. **Reward the top 10% with a loyalty programme.** These 40 customers bring INR 1.08M (36.2% of revenue) and none is at churn risk today, so the goal is to keep them. Offer early access to festive sales and track their order frequency monthly.
2. **Run a win-back offer for the 35 customers inactive for 90+ days.** Their past spend is about INR 96k, and 94% of them are low spenders, so use a low-cost email or coupon rather than expensive discounts. Measure success as the share who order again within 30 days.
3. **Plan stock and campaigns around October to December, led by Electronics.** Q4 is the strongest quarter and Electronics the biggest category, so secure Electronics inventory and ad budget before October, and plan a December follow-up to soften the 10% drop after November.

## Next steps
- Look at the middle 40% of customers, who bring the largest block of revenue (43.5%), for upsell opportunities.
- Replace the simulated data with real order history and re-run the notebook to confirm these patterns.
- Track category mix by month to see whether Electronics is the driver of the Q4 peak.

*Assumption: the dataset is simulated with a built-in festive-season lift, so the Q4 pattern reflects that design. The method carries over to real data; the numbers do not.*
